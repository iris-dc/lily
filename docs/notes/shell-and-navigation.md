# Shell, navigation and the composition root

> The tabs and their retitling, `AppNavigation`, where things go in the composition root. Moved out of `README.md` and `CLAUDE.md` on 2026-10-17 and kept as written. The reference part describes the feature as the README did; the dated notes record decisions, lessons and the evidence behind them, newest last, so an older sentence can be superseded by a later one or by a marker such as "(removed 2026-10-14)". Read this file before touching the area; the rules that apply everywhere are in `AGENTS.md`.

## Reference (from the README)

- Tabbed shell (Home, Explore, Chats, Profile; Home is the caller's groups and games, Explore the public feed with the groups to discover above it, Chats the inbox and the group rooms; the Groups tab went on 2026-09-28 and Chats arrived on 2026-09-29). The tab icons are light outline glyphs (`house`, `binoculars`, `message`, `person`), chosen by eye from rendered variants on 2026-09-29 and drawn from `UIImage` symbols because the iOS 26 tab bar honours no other weight (see `MainTabView`). Explore switches between a list (with distance from the user) and a map that opens on the user's position at the filter's radius, whatever the criteria match, with one glass pin per matching event; selecting a pin shows a preview card that opens the event detail. A filter panel drops down from the Explore toolbar and narrows list and map by event type (multi-select, including "Other"), distance from the user's position (10 km by default), a maximum price you type in, skill level, a date range and open spots; the filter is remembered between launches (everything but the date range, which would go stale; `-reset-session` forgets it), and the backend ranks the feed around the position the app sends. An event may carry optional details: a description, who the host is looking for, a skill level and a per-person price; the detail screen shows whichever are set, and list cards and preview cards show the price of paid games. Home asks a guest to sign in instead of requesting user-scoped lists.

- **The tab bar slides away and back with a room's push and pop (2026-10-18, on the user's report that the bar "just appears" when a chat is left).** Before, `hidesTabBarOnCompact()` set `toolbarVisibility(.hidden, for: .tabBar)` on the room and the inbox themselves, and a pushed screen's preference leaves with the screen: a 30 fps recording of the committed build showed the room sliding out over an empty bottom for about a quarter second and the bar then drawn whole in one frame, and vanishing in one frame on the push. Flipping that preference inside `withAnimation` from the screen's `onAppear`/`onDisappear` (the first attempt) cannot help, because `onDisappear` fires after the pop has finished. Now the stack root owns the visibility: `animatesTabBar(depth:)` (`Core/UI/Layout/TabBarHiding.swift`) on the Home and Chats `NavigationStack`s puts a `TabBarHiding` registry and the path's count (`\.navigationDepth`) in the environment, `hidesTabBarOnCompact()` registers the screen's depth there on its first appearance (`hide(_:at:)` keeps the first depth: a screen appearing again after the pop of what covered it may read a stale one), the root forgets every registration deeper than the path's count when the path shrinks (`prune(above:)`: a popped screen, while a covered screen keeps its place, so the bar hides again when the group detail pushed over a room is popped; the first cut released on disappear instead and the review's recording caught the bar staying over the room after that pop), and the root sets `toolbarVisibility` from `hides(at: depth) && !layoutMode.isRegular` inside `withAnimation(.easeInOut(duration: DesignTokens.Duration.normal))` whenever that value changes, so a pop (the path shrinks), a push over a room (the new top has no registration) and a room's own push all flip the bar as the transition starts (superseded 2026-10-26, below: a screen pushed over a room keeps the bar hidden). Two screens at one depth hold separate tokens and either keeps the bar hidden; the path is only ever appended to or shrunk, never replaced at the same count, which is what lets a registration stand for the screen at its depth (`TabBarHidingTests`). A stack without the root modifier (Explore, Profile, the split view's detail, where the bar never hides anyway) keeps the old static visibility. Evidence (`/tmp/lily-tabbar`, 2026-10-18): the strip under the content measured per frame (`signalstats` YAVG) moved in one frame at the pop in the committed build and over five to eight frames in the new one, and the contact sheets showed the bar fading in under the room as it slides out and fading out as a room slides in, and a second recording (room, its group detail pushed over it, back, back) showed the bar returning with the detail's push, hiding again with the pop back onto the room and returning with the room's pop; the system picks the bar's own fade, shorter than the `withAnimation` duration asked for.

An iOS 26 tab bar built at launch, in view from its first frame, places its selection pill on the first tab and slides it to the selected one over its first frames, whatever the selection was when it was built (the 2026-10-26 language note recorded the same for a bar built while the app is on screen; the one-bar change of 605e6e9 covers a bar built under the landing, which a sign-in reveals settled, not the bar a cached guest's or user's cold launch shows at once). Two variants were tried on a scratch iPhone 17 Pro with 30 fps recordings and the tab bar cropped from every frame into one sheet (`/tmp/pill-probe`): a `.transaction { $0.disablesAnimations = true }` on the `TabView` changed nothing (the pill under Home in the bar's first frame, between the tabs in the next two, on Explore in the fourth, as in the baseline); UIKit's animations switched off from the app delegate until the shell's first frame had committed put the pill under Explore in the bar's first, faint frame, the Home glyph white from the start. So `LaunchAnimations` (`Core/UI/Effects`): `freeze()` from `LilyAppDelegate.application(_:didFinishLaunchingWithOptions:)` calls `UIView.setAnimationsEnabled(false)`; `resumeAfterFirstFrame()` from `AppRootView.onAppear` turns them back on in the next turn of the main run loop, after the frame being built has committed; `AppConfig.Launch.animationsResumeFallback` (3 s, longer than a phone's cold start) turns them on regardless, since an app without UIKit animations would be a worse fault than the slide. SwiftUI's own animations (the reveal, the crossfades) are not UIKit's and run as before; nothing else of ours animates in the first frame. Test: `LaunchAnimationsTests`.
